How this is worked out, and its limits

Supply by place. Every unit in the Balancing Mechanism reports a Physical Notification (its planned output, minute by minute). Where the system operator has instructed a unit to change (a bid-offer acceptance, e.g. curtailing Scottish wind), the accepted level is used instead. Units are located from the open Power Station Dictionary, supplemented by hand for newer plant; offshore wind is drawn at sea and assigned to the region where it comes ashore. These outputs are scaled so the total matches Elexon's 5-minute metered generation (FUELINST).

Landscape view. A purely indicative picture: hills where more electricity is made than used nearby, valleys where more is used than made, so power runs downhill. Each station's output is placed where it is (offshore wind at sea, imports where they land), small local generation is spread evenly over its region, and each region's estimated demand is spread 80% by where people live (towns of 1,000+ from GeoNames) and 20% evenly. Offshore wind farms are drawn as narrow spikes at sea, with their cables, and their output is counted where it comes ashore. The difference is smoothed over about 35 km and drawn on a fixed scale, with contours at fixed steps, so the landscape can be compared from one half-hour to the next. The smoothing distance shapes the picture; the regional totals behind it are the same as on the flow map. The 3D version uses the same surface with heights exaggerated, drawn with three.js.

Money in / out. An accounting picture of who pays whom for one half-hour, not a record of actual bills. Each region pays the market index price for the electricity it uses and is paid it for what it generates; imports are paid abroad, exports paid for from abroad, so the money runs against the power and adds up to zero. On top: Contract for Difference payments, estimated as (strike price − market price) × output for each unit with a live contract (current indexed strike prices from the Low Carbon Contracts Company; negative when the price is above strike and generators pay back; about 1.3 GW of small distribution-connected contracts not counted), and balancing payments to particular units. Both are recovered from all consumers in proportion to use, so each region pays a share. Real bills use hedged contract prices, network charges and other levies not shown here.

Small, local generation. Only stations metered in the Balancing Mechanism appear as circles. Most onshore wind farms and solar parks (Delabole, Carland Cross and Fullabrook in the South West, for example) connect to local distribution networks and have no live metering, so they are not drawn individually. Their output is not missing: NESO's embedded wind and solar estimates (national totals) are shared between regions in proportion to an indicative capacity distribution and counted in each region's supply; in the Landscape view they are spread evenly over each region.

Demand by place. National demand is everything generated plus net imports, less storage charging. It is shared between the 14 distribution areas using suppliers' own half-hourly demand notifications for each area, which gives a live regional shape.

Flows. Each region's surplus or deficit is passed across a reduced 23-path network with a DC power-flow approximation, using rough transfer capabilities as weights. That gives a pattern of flow, not the flow on any real circuit. The Western HVDC link is treated as an ordinary path, although in reality it is set by the operator.

"Where does it come from?" Power does not travel from one station to one customer. The breakdown uses proportional sharing (Bialek's flow tracing): at each region, incoming power is assumed to mix evenly before moving on. It is an accounting convention, not a measurement.

Prices. Britain has one national wholesale price, not regional ones. The market index price is the volume-weighted price of short-term trading on the power exchange for each half-hour; the imbalance (cash-out) price is what suppliers and generators pay or receive for being out of balance. Most electricity is sold through private contracts whose prices are not published.

Balancing payments. Every unit in the Balancing Mechanism publishes the prices at which it will increase output (offers) or reduce it (bids), and the system operator's instructions are public. Elexon's indicative cashflows give what each unit was paid, or paid back, for those instructions in each half-hour. Wind farms usually have negative bid prices (they lose subsidy when switched off), so they are paid to reduce output; a unit with a positive bid price pays it back when turned down, because it had already sold that power in the market and no longer has to make it (for gas plant the bid roughly reflects the fuel saved; a wind farm saves no fuel but still returns the value of power it sold). Cashflows are published about half an hour after the period, so live view shows the latest published half-hour.

Check against NESO. NESO publishes forecast day-ahead flows across major boundaries (before constraint actions). Scotland→England (B6/SCOTEX) is the most useful comparison: when the model shows less than that forecast, the gap is mostly curtailed Scottish wind.
