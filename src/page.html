How this is worked out, and its limits

Supply by place. Every unit in the Balancing Mechanism reports a Physical Notification (its planned output, minute by minute). Where the system operator has instructed a unit to change (a bid-offer acceptance, e.g. curtailing Scottish wind), the accepted level is used instead. Units are located from the open Power Station Dictionary, supplemented by hand for newer plant; offshore wind is assigned to the region where it lands. These outputs are scaled so the total matches Elexon's 5-minute metered generation (FUELINST).

Small, local generation. NESO's embedded wind and solar estimates (national totals) are shared between regions in proportion to an indicative capacity distribution.

Demand by place. National demand is everything generated plus net imports, less storage charging. It is shared between the 14 distribution areas using suppliers' own half-hourly demand notifications for each area, which gives a live regional shape.

Flows. Each region's surplus or deficit is passed across a reduced 23-path network with a DC power-flow approximation, using rough transfer capabilities as weights. That gives a pattern of flow, not the flow on any real circuit. The Western HVDC link is treated as an ordinary path, although in reality it is set by the operator.

"Where does it come from?" Power does not travel from one station to one customer. The breakdown uses proportional sharing (Bialek's flow tracing): at each region, incoming power is assumed to mix evenly before moving on. It is an accounting convention, not a measurement.

Check against NESO. NESO publishes forecast day-ahead flows across major boundaries (before constraint actions). Scotland→England (B6/SCOTEX) is the most useful comparison: when the model shows less than that forecast, the gap is mostly curtailed Scottish wind.
